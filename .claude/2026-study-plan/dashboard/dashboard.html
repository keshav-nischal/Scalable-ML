    Why this survives the AI boom

    re-read when the doubt returns — don't reopen it

    "Do something else" isn't an exit from AI risk — it's a worse seat in the same theatre. The real question was never ML vs. something else; it's be someone who commands AI, or someone disrupted by it.

    The plan aims at evaluation, testing, reliability, integration, production trust — deliberately countercyclical: the better and more prolific AI gets, the more AI-generated systems exist, and the more someone is needed to verify they're correct, safe, and trustworthy. AI capability and demand for people who can tell whether AI output actually works rise together. The boom is your market: the paid-for last 20% — reliability, eval, security, integration — is exactly the wedge.

    If AI plateaus — strong tool needing operatorsSkilled builders win → you win big
    If AI is transformative — commodity dev automatedValue → judgment, eval, trust → still your wedge, better placed
    If full AGI — economy breaksNo career plan matters → moot for everyone

    Across every future where your choices matter, this plan is best-in-class; in the one where they don't, nothing beats it. It holds on one condition the plan already enforces: stay above the automatable line — deploy, evaluate, differentiate, own reliability. Never tutorial notebooks or commodity work.

    read once, live by them
    The 8 rules
    • Ship > perfect. A deployed 85% beats a local 99%. Deploy, then improve.
    • Deploy every project. No demo URL = didn't happen.
    • Quantify everything. "faithfulness 0.62 → 0.89", "640ms → 95ms". Numbers are credibility.
    • Lead with the wedge. Show the testing/eval others skip.
    • Trim ruthlessly. 4–6 hrs/chapter. Note the rabbit hole, move on.
    • Core vs. stretch. Behind? Drop stretch — never the anchors, SAA, or Ch 15.
    • Post from the work, not on top of it. Every session leaves a breadcrumb.
    • Protect momentum over completeness. Missing Ch 19 is fine. Missing P3 is not.
